# Lorenz Attractor

## 1. Quick presentation

The Lorenz attractor is a three-dimensional dynamical system introduced by Edward Lorenz while studying atmospheric convection. It is defined by the equations

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=\sigma(y-x),\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt}=x(\rho-z)-y,\qquad
\frac{dz}{dt}=xy-\beta z.
\]

For the commonly used parameters \(\sigma=10\), \(\rho=28\), and \(\beta=8/3\), the trajectory forms a characteristic butterfly-shaped structure. The system is deterministic, but it is highly sensitive to its initial conditions: two trajectories starting almost at the same point can eventually follow very different paths.

### Why is it so popular?

- It is one of the clearest examples of deterministic chaos.
- Its equations are short and easy to simulate numerically.
- It demonstrates the butterfly effect and sensitivity to initial conditions.
- Its visualization makes abstract ideas such as attractors, instability, and bounded motion intuitive.
- It is useful in mathematics, physics, meteorology, nonlinear dynamics, and scientific programming.

## 3. Effect of initial conditions

An initial condition is the starting point \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\). In a chaotic system, a very small change in this point may be almost invisible at the beginning but becomes noticeable as time increases. The trajectories may first overlap and later separate, visit different lobes at different times, and produce distinct long-term paths.

The attractor remains bounded for suitable parameters, so the trajectories do not normally escape to infinity. However, the exact path is not predictable for long times without knowing the initial condition with extremely high precision.
